# Hackers in the Loop — Brand Guidelines

## Brand core

**Name:** Hackers in the Loop  
**Short name:** HITL  
**Descriptor:** Open source for agentic systems.  
**Brand promise:** Keep intelligence hackable.  
**Positioning:** An open-source hackerspace for the systems beneath agentic software.

Hackers in the Loop exists to keep the machinery of agentic computing open, inspectable, composable, and in human hands. The community builds shared infrastructure, conducts research in public, and helps contributors turn experiments into durable open-source systems.

## Logo system

The identity is a typographic workshop stamp: **HACK** stacked over **HITL**. “HACK” states the community’s posture. “HITL” is both the organization’s short name and a reference to human-in-the-loop systems.

The horizontal coral rule ends in a circular node. This is the **signal line**: the point of intervention, active contribution, and human agency.

Use the supplied artwork rather than rebuilding the wordmark from live type. The production logo files contain outlined Helvetica Neue Bold and Menlo Bold geometry, so they cannot substitute fonts in browsers or design tools.

### Logo variants

- **Primary badge:** Includes the descriptor. Use at 256 px or larger.
- **Compact badge:** Removes the descriptor. Use from 64–255 px.
- **Signal mark:** Use for favicons and UI marks from 16–63 px.
- **Horizontal lockup:** Uses the badge with the full organization name. Prefer it when the audience may not recognize HITL.
- **One-color:** The complete badge may be reproduced entirely in black or white when color is unavailable.

### Clear space

Keep clear space equal to the diameter of the signal node around every side of the badge or lockup. Do not place text, borders, or other marks inside this area.

### Do not

- Rotate, skew, stretch, outline, or add shadows to the badge.
- Recolor the signal node independently from its rule.
- Add gradients, glows, circuit textures, brains, robots, or AI sparkles.
- Turn the letterforms into an illegible monogram or rotational/pinwheel shape.
- Use the small descriptor where it cannot be read.

## Color

### Core palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Typical use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Primary | Carbon | `#111315` | Badge, headings, dark surfaces |
| Ground | Warm Paper | `#F4F2EA` | Main page and poster background |
| Surface | White | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, documentation, working surfaces |
| Accent | Signal | `#FF553D` | Rule, node, active state, intervention |

### Supporting palette

| Name | Hex | Typical use |
|---|---:|---|
| Graphite | `#555B58` | Body text and secondary labels |
| Alloy | `#AEB3B0` | Metadata on dark surfaces |
| Fog | `#D8DCD9` | Borders and inactive structures |
| Deep Signal | `#C8321E` | Small accent text on light backgrounds |
| Canvas | `#EDEDE9` | Brand boards and large neutral fields |

Aim for approximately **55% Carbon, 25% Warm Paper, 15% White, and 5% Signal** across a composition. The ratio is a discipline, not a mathematical requirement.

Signal is a graphic accent rather than body-text color. Use Deep Signal where accessible small red text is required. Carbon on Warm Paper is the default high-contrast reading pair.

## Typography

### Display and headlines

Use a bold neo-grotesk with compact tracking. The master artwork uses **Helvetica Neue Bold** with custom spacing. For open-web work, **Inter Tight ExtraBold** is the preferred substitute.

- Sentence case for most headlines.
- All caps for short declarations, event titles, and identity components.
- Tight tracking on large display text; never compress the font horizontally.

### Body and interface

Use **Helvetica Neue Regular/Medium**. For open-web work, use **Inter**.

Body language should be plain, direct, and specific. Avoid futurist claims, marketing superlatives, and vague talk about “revolutionizing AI.”

### Technical metadata

Use **Menlo** for labels, code, dates, project states, specifications, and navigation metadata. For open-web work, use **IBM Plex Mono**.

Monospaced type is a supporting texture, not the primary personality. The brand should not imitate a terminal.

## Graphic language

### Signal line

The horizontal coral rule and terminal node are the primary reusable device.

Use it to:

- underline decisions and active states;
- connect projects or steps without enclosing them;
- mark where a person intervenes in a system;
- lead the eye through diagrams, posters, and interfaces.

Prefer one signal line per composition. Keep the rule horizontal in identity work. In technical diagrams it may follow an actual path, but should never form a decorative rotational symbol.

### Layout

- Use an 8 px base grid.
- Prefer two-level stacks: title/detail, project/endorsement, system/human.
- Use generous margins and a small number of large working surfaces.
- Use square or lightly rounded cards. Avoid pill-heavy interfaces.
- Let black fields behave like physical labels or equipment plates.

### Diagrams

- Carbon lines and square computational nodes.
- Signal red only for human intervention, active execution, or the current path.
- Neutral Fog for inactive paths.
- No gradients or decorative neural-network webs.

### Photography

Favor documentary images of real work: hardware benches, notebooks, terminals, soldering, whiteboards, contributors, and unfinished prototypes. Use warm natural light and honest texture. Avoid humanoid robots, glowing brains, floating holograms, and generic stock “AI” imagery.

## Project architecture

Hackers in the Loop is the community umbrella. Projects retain their own names and may use their own secondary accent, while carrying one of these endorsements:

- **A Hackers in the Loop project**
- **Built in the Hackers in the Loop ecosystem**
- **Stewarded by Hackers in the Loop**

The HITL badge acts as a flag of stewardship, not a corporate product badge.

## Voice

The voice is technically serious, hands-on, generous, and plainspoken.

### Say

- Keep intelligence hackable.
- Understand the system. Improve the system. Share what you learn.
- Built in the open.
- Bring code, questions, and hardware.
- Here is how it works.
- Here is where we need help.

### Avoid

- Democratizing the future of intelligence.
- Revolutionary AI innovation.
- Unlocking limitless possibilities.
- The autonomous workforce of tomorrow.

## Core applications

- **Website:** Use the horizontal lockup in navigation, the brand promise as the hero statement, and the descriptor in the supporting copy.
- **Repositories:** Lead with the project name and add “A Hackers in the Loop project.”
- **Events:** Use large black display type, Warm Paper fields, and a single Signal line.
- **Community avatars:** Use the compact badge or Signal mark—never the full badge with unreadable descriptor text.
- **Hardware:** Treat the identity as an equipment plate with monospaced specifications and a visible Signal line.
